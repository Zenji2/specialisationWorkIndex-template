Information Design - Mapping Prototypes

Prototype 1: Cartesian Mapping

For the first prototype, I swapped the axis of the mapping where the y position controls filter frequency while the x position controls the amount of reverb. This creates a simple two-axis control space where the position of the mouse depicts how much tone and reverb there is. This prototype shows the direct spatial relationship between mouse position and sound manipulation and was my initial idea when I thought of the extended technique.

Prototype 2: Radial Mapping

This prototype replaces the x and y axis with a circular mapping system where the closer you get to the centre the tone gets duller, and further is brighter, while the angle around the centre controls the reverb. This allows tone and reverb to still be controlled by the mouse but through a different spatial model, and explores whether circular movement is a more efficient interaction for the mouse player.

Prototype 3: Movement Mapping

For this prototype, I looked at the speed of the mouse player rather than just cursor position. The horizontal position still controls the filter frequency, while the speed of the mouse movement controls the reverb. If the mouse player moves the cursor slower, it will produce less reverb, and if they swipe it quickly it produces more. This explores whether velocity can also help shape the sound, rather than just only the mouse position.

Prototype 4: Grid Mapping

This prototype is quite different to the rest, where it replaces the fine control of the mouse position with concrete blocks. The canvas is divided into a grid, with each cell having a fixed amount of filter frequency and reverb. When the mouse player moves between the cells, it will produced an equal increased amount rather than continuous transitions. This explores a more constrained form of spatial control, where the mouse player has limited choice and not as much freedom with mouse positioning.

Information Design - Feedback Prototypes

Prototype 5: Movement Trail

This prototype shows feedback through a fading trail following the mouse cursor. Every time the mouse moves, it will leave a visual mark which will allow the player to see both their current position and past position. Nothing has changed regarding audio mapping but I have not used any layout lines.

Prototype 6: Blob Sound Visualisation

This prototype removes the need for cursor position feedback and instead visualises the current sound using a circle. The filter frequency changes the size of the circle, whereas reverb changes the amount of glow surrounding it. This explores a different type of visualised feedback where it can communicate the result of the mouse player's actions without the need for a movement trail or text.

Prototype 7: Explicit Feedback

The feedback for this prototype is a bit different to the other two, where it directly communicates the current state of the mouse player's actions using a visual meter/bar and numerical values. Rather than requiring the players to interpret visual feedback, this prototype makes it much more explicit making the filter frequency and reverb visible. This explores a more informational approach to feedback.
