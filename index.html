TWOGETHER

Brief Synopsis

TWOGETHER is a browser based, two-player collaborative audio instrument and rhythm game. It requires two players to play, with one player using the keyboard, and the other using the mouse. The keyboard player can press the keyboard letters from A-L to play a note, while the mouse player uses mouse movement to change the pitch of that note. There are circles in a grid format that show the players what each note will play referencing the keyboard letter "A".

Concept Evolution

During the initial exploration phases in assignment 1 and 2, the original concept/idea was to make a two-player toy synthesiser. In the parallel prototypes, I looked at different layouts and mappings that would improve how my audio instrument looked and worked. Following peer user testing and critical evaluation of my audio instrument, I decided to make a major change to it.

I decided to replace the filter and reverb with a pitch and octave change instead due to it being a better change for audio making. I found that reverb and filter doesn't really change much when it comes to audio, and I found it a weak regarding my concept. If I really wanted the players to work together to create a new sound, I needed to change the octaves and semitones instead so they aren't limited to just 9 notes.

I also decided to make the pivot to making it into a game, on top of being a free play two-player synth. As I thought about how to make the characterisation "Collaboration" more obvious in my audio instrument, I wanted to think of a way they could work together towards a common goal, rather than just free playing and making different notes. I decided to add three songs to my audio instrument that the players can replicate, working together to hit the orbs and score points (and finish the song). This required both players to coordinate their spatial positioning and timing.

Design Scenario

Prompt: Design a synthesizer to be operated by two or more people using a single computer.

Context: Instruments (and increasingly computational devices) are typically understood as being designed to be operated by one person at a time. While many environments exist for playing these alongside others in physical or virtual spaces, there are limited examples of browser-based instruments exploring what a locally cooperative or split screen approach might produce.

My Project Response - TWOGETHER

TWOGETHER AUDIO INSTRUMENT - CLICK HERE TO PLAY

My project responds to the scenario by allowing two players their own individual role, and can collaborate together to create a different sound. Without the keyboard input, the mouse player cannot play the sound, and without the mouse input, the keyboard player is stuck with the original notes. This shows that neither player can complete the musical game independently, satisfying the design scenario of a local cooperative browser instrument.

Core Interactive Features

2D Pitch Grid: I divided the canvas into a 5x3 grid, using the previous mapping from a parallel prototype as reference on how to dynamically change the pitch for each tile area.

Cooperative Rhythm Game: Three songs can be selected and played, generating orbs that would appear on the note needed to play it. A new orb will generate on the next note needed, and continues until the song is over.

Visual HUD feedback: There is an animated score counter, dynamic floating success/miss popups, and a ring that closes to visually show how much time to the players until it disappears. There is also the score and notes in the header that changes as the song goes on, allowing players to track how many notes and score they are on mid-game. Above the screen keyboard letters also shows dynamically changing X and Y feedback, allowing players another way to see how the mouse movement has changed the note.

Freeplay: Allow users to play the audio instrument without needing to touch the game part. This allows both players to test the controls, or even make their own music by working together to create different notes that are unachievable individually. I also allowed the mouse player to click on the on screen keyboard letters to test out the keys.

Game Controls: The players can pause, resume, and restart the game whenever they feel like. If they need to take a break in the middle of a game, they can just hit the pause button and when they click resume it starts at the note they paused at rather than from the start. Users can also click the listen button to see what the song sounds like before playing. They can also learn how to play the song in freeplay mode as it displays the notes in listening mode.

Extended Technique: Spatial Input - mousemove event

Rather than just doing a simple point and click pointer function, I utilised spatial input via the mousemove event through continiously tracking the cursor coordinates across the canvas to change the pitch in real time.

As the mouse player moves the cursor across the canvas, the mousemove listener captures the offsetX and offsetY coordinates. These coordinates are then mapped to the pitch grid, allowing the change in pitch in both X and Y axis.

Responsive and Adaptive Design System

I decided to make my visual aesthetic styled around a lo-fi twilight city landscape, using grey tones and orange accents. I also used glow and neon to make the colours pop out, contrasting against the background and make it easier for the users to see.

For screen size adapability, I made TWOGETHER adapt across the following desktop screen sizes:

Compact/Medium (840px and under)

Expanded (840px-1199px)

Large (1200px-1600px)

Extra Large(>1600px)
